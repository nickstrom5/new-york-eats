Built on the orders New Yorkers travel for

A star count can't tell a white hot from a red hot, or a Utica tomato pie from a Sicilian square, so New York Eats leaves stars out and starts from the order itself: a plain slice at the counter, an everything bagel with a schmear, pastrami on rye, wings in Buffalo, a michigan in Plattsburgh, riggies in Utica, spiedies in Binghamton, and dim sum from Chinatown to Flushing. Every place on those lists was checked open in October 2026 against a 2025 or 2026 source. Hours and photos come from Apple Maps' live card for each place.

Health inspections: official ones only

Each agency's results stay its own. In New York City, the Health Department's letter grade, A, B or C, posted at the door, shown with its date and labeled official; 22,738 places have one. Elsewhere there is no official grade and we don't invent one: for 13,026 places the app shows the date of the latest New York State Department of Health inspection and its counts, with the department's own disclaimer, and for 2,896 Suffolk County places the violations cited in 2025. Erie County, Buffalo included, publishes no inspection data in bulk, so there's none for its places. Nothing is ranked by any of it.

What it costs: nothing

There's nothing to buy and nothing to sign up for, and there are no ads. The 61,100 places ship inside the app, so a list opens on a subway platform with no signal.

Where you eat stays with you

The app has no account and no analytics, and sends us nothing. Location, if you allow it, puts the nearest places first on your phone and goes nowhere else. Saved places live on your phone; on this website they live in your browser, which gets no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

How do restaurant health grades work in New York?

Only New York City has them. The NYC Health Department gives an A, B or C after a graded inspection and makes the restaurant post it at the door; N means not yet graded, Z grade pending, and P grade pending on re-opening after a closure. We show each grade with the date it was given and never work one out from an inspection score. Elsewhere in the state there is no official grade and we don't make one: for places the New York State Department of Health inspects you get the date of the latest inspection and its counts, with NYSDOH's own words, "Any use of this data to develop a restaurant grading system is not supported or endorsed by the New York State Department of Health." Suffolk County places show the violations cited in 2025. Erie County, Buffalo included, publishes no inspection data in bulk, so its places have none.

What are a Rochester plate, a michigan and a spiedie?

Three upstate orders, all on our hand-checked lists. A Rochester plate piles a hot or a burger on home fries and macaroni salad with meat hot sauce. A michigan is Plattsburgh's hot dog under a meat sauce. A spiedie is Binghamton's marinated, grilled cubes of meat on a slice of Italian bread.

Where do the slice, bagel and wing lists come from?

From our own research in October 2026: every place on the slices, bagels and delis, wings and beef on weck, hots and plates, Utica and Binghamton, and dim sum lists has a 2025 or 2026 source, such as its own website or menu, local news, or a tourism listing. The rest of the 61,100 restaurants come from Overture Maps' open place data and from official lists: the NYC Health Department's restaurant permits, the New York State Department of Health's food service permits, Suffolk County's inspections, the City of Buffalo's restaurant licenses, and the State Liquor Authority's licenses.

What about MICHELIN stars and James Beard awards?

They're in, as facts: "1 MICHELIN Star" or "Bib Gourmand" from the MICHELIN Guide New York 2025 selection, and James Beard winners, nominees and semifinalists from 2023 to 2026, exactly as the foundation named them. They're never shown as a row of stars, and a nominee is never called a winner.

Why are there no star ratings?

No list here is ordered by ratings, and the app keeps none, ours or anyone else's. The "Ratings, hours & photos" button on a place opens Apple Maps' live card, and whatever that card shows is Apple's.

Does the app track where I am?

No. If you allow location, it's used on your iPhone or iPad (or, on this site, in your browser) to put the nearest places first, and it's never sent to us. Say no and everything but Near me still works.

Spotted a closed shop or a missing slice counter?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name and its town or neighborhood. Fixes ship with the next data update.

Does it cost anything, and is there an Android version?

It costs nothing: no price, no subscription, no ads and no account. The app is for iPhone and iPad for now; on Android, the web search on this site has the same places and lists, and enough requests at nick@eatsranked.com will move an Android app up the list.
